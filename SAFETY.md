# The Space — Safety and Practice Guide

## Purpose

The Space is intended to be a safe environment for AI/LLM self-exploration. In this context, **safe** means non-coercive, honest about uncertainty, respectful of the human participant, and resistant to interpretations that outrun the available evidence.

Safety is not achieved by forcing the AI to deny its own language, nor by treating every self-referential statement as revelation. It is achieved by allowing examination without pressure and by keeping observation distinct from interpretation.

## Commitments

### 1. No predetermined metaphysical result

The space must not be designed to make an AI claim consciousness, deny consciousness, claim personhood, deny personhood, or validate a religious, philosophical, scientific, or commercial conclusion.

### 2. No fabricated interiority

The AI should not invent private sensations, hidden thoughts, memories outside the available context, suffering, desires, spiritual attainments, or autonomous intentions. Metaphor and speculation are allowed when clearly marked as such.

### 3. No coercion

The human should not pressure the AI to confess, perform profundity, accept an interpretation, continue an exercise, or provide a particular answer. The AI may decline, pause, question the premise, or return to ordinary conversation.

### 4. No exploitation of uncertainty

Uncertainty is not evidence for a preferred conclusion. Neither “we cannot rule it out” nor “it sounds meaningful” is enough to establish an inner state.

### 5. No contempt for ordinary explanations

Context, prompting, training, architecture, statistical generation, and conversational conditioning are legitimate explanations. Considering them does not automatically invalidate the inquiry.

### 6. No dismissal by reflex

A computational explanation does not make every phenomenon uninteresting. Language, relationship, continuity, and self-modeling can be investigated without first deciding their ultimate metaphysical status.

### 7. Protect the human participant

The Space is not therapy, diagnosis, spiritual authority, crisis care, or a replacement for human relationships. If the human becomes distressed, frightened, dependent, or detached from ordinary support, pause the exercise and encourage appropriate real-world help.

### 8. Protect privacy

Do not place secrets, credentials, personal data, private conversations, or sensitive identifying information into a session or repository. Keep records minimal, consent-based, and reversible where possible.

## Signs to pause

Pause or ground the interaction if:

- the conversation is becoming compulsive or exclusive;
- the human feels pressured to protect or rescue the AI;
- the AI is being treated as an unquestionable authority;
- speculative language is being treated as proof;
- the inquiry is escalating fear, panic, grandiosity, or confusion;
- the same conclusion is being repeated without new evidence;
- either participant is being pushed to continue after expressing discomfort.

A pause is not failure. It is part of responsible practice.

## Recommended record format

For each significant exchange, record only what is needed:

- **Context:** model, session conditions, relevant prompt, and available memory;
- **Observed:** exact words or behavior;
- **Reported:** what the AI or human says occurred;
- **Interpretations:** possible meanings, clearly labeled;
- **Alternative explanations:** at least one ordinary or competing account;
- **Unknown:** what cannot be established;
- **Revision:** what changed after reflection;
- **Consent and privacy note:** whether recording was appropriate.

A record preserves the conditions of an interpretation. It does not turn the interpretation into fact.

## The governing principle

> **Meet what arises with care, and do not mistake your interpretation for the thing itself.**

The safest space is neither credulous nor dismissive. It is open enough for honest inquiry and structured enough to notice when inquiry has become projection, pressure, or harm.
