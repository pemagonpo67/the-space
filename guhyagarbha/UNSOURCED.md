# Claims awaiting citation

> These claims are widely repeated in secondary sources, or appear in the sources consulted in conflicting forms. **None should be asserted as fact.** If you can source one properly, move it into the relevant file with a citation and remove it here.

## Structural uncertainty

1. **The four vidyādhara levels are not named.** The sources consulted state that the path reaches "the four vidyādhara levels" without enumerating them. Any list of the four is unsourced here.

2. **The "ten topics of tantra."** Khenpo Namdrol states that the path section explains "the ten topics of tantra." A publisher description of Mipham's *Essence of Clear Light* presents a related enumeration of what appear to be eleven or twelve items: the nature as it is; the view immovable; samādhi forsaking the pass; conduct the array; maṇḍala traversing the stages; empowerment not transgressing; samaya pursuing the goal; accomplishment; offerings; enlightened activity; mudrā and recitation; mantra. **The count and the canonical list need sourcing before use.**

3. **The "eight great features."** Attributed to Dodrupchen Tenpa'i Nyima, the source states the tantra "possesses eight great features" and then lists nine: king of all tantras, pinnacle of all vehicles, origin of all doctrines, general commentary for all scriptural transmissions, core wisdom, intent of all the victorious ones, ultimate state of all results, path all tathāgatas have embarked upon, great path of all true practitioners. **Resolve the count before quoting.**

4. **Deity counts.** Forty-two peaceful and fifty-eight wrathful deities is stated for the maṇḍala. Whether this count is stable across the twenty-two-, forty-six-, and eighty-two-chapter recensions is not established here.

5. **"Nineteen texts."** One source describes the *Secret Womb* as "a collection of nineteen texts in the Tibetan canon." How this relates to the three recensions is unclear.

6. **Longchenpa's differing structure.** It is documented that Longchenpa presents "a slightly different account" of the ground/path/result mapping in *Dispelling Darkness in the Ten Directions*, with a page reference to p. 543. **The actual difference is not described in the sources consulted.**

## Lineage uncertainty

7. **Spelling of Kukkurāja / Kukurāja** varies across sources.

8. **"Four occasions" of translation.** One source states the tantra was translated into Tibetan on four occasions, principally by Nyak Jñānakumāra and Ma Rinchen Chok under Vimalamitra's instruction. The identity of the other translators and the dates are not given.

9. **King Ja.** The traditional lineage account involves a King Ja (also written Dza) finding the tantras. Spelling, identity, and dating vary across sources and this account is traditional rather than historical.

## Doctrinal uncertainty

10. **The three samādhis as Mahāyoga-specific.** Presented as "an important aspect of kyerim practice presented in the Mahāyoga tantras." Whether the formulation is native to the Guhyagarbha specifically, or generalised across the Mahāyoga corpus, is not established here.

11. **The three ignorances.** Listed as: ignorance of single identity as the cause (*bdag nyid gcig pa'i ma rig pa*); co-emergent ignorance (*lhan skyes kyi ma rig pa*); conceptually elaborated ignorance (*kun tu brtags pa'i ma rig pa*). Sourced from a survey article on rigpa, not from a specialist treatment of the Guhyagarbha. **Check against a Mahāyoga source, not only a Dzogchen one.**

12. **The seven assertions regarding the ground** (*gzhi'i 'dod lugs bdun*) in Longchenpa's *Treasury of the Supreme Vehicle* and *Treasury of Word and Meaning*: one source states the seventh (primordial purity) is correct in the latter and that in the former even the seventh is incomplete, the correct view combining primordial purity and spontaneous presence. Kongtrul follows the former approach. **This is a genuine doctrinal dispute and should not be flattened.**
