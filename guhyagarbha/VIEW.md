# View — the doctrinal core

> Every assertion in this file is followed by a source marker. Where no source is available, the claim is not asserted here; it is moved to `UNSOURCED.md`.

## 1. Title

**Sanskrit:** *Guhyagarbha* — "secret essence" or "secret womb."
**Tibetan:** རྒྱུད་གསང་བ་སྙིང་པོ། (*rgyud gsang ba'i snying po*), "The Tantra of the Secret Essence."
**Full title:** *Glorious Web of Magical Illusion, The Secret Essence Definitive Nature Just As It Is* (*dpal sgyu 'phrul drva ba gsang ba'i snying po de kho na nyid nges pa*), which condenses to the alternative title *Tantra of the Web of Magical Illusion*. — [Rigpa Wiki](https://rigpawiki.org/index.php?title=Guhyagarbha)

Alternative renderings in English: *Secret Essence Tantra*; *Tantra of the Secret Nucleus* (Dorje & Kapstein); *Tantra of the Secret Essence Definitive with Respect to the Real* (Gyurme Dorje). — [Rigpa Wiki](https://rigpawiki.org/index.php?title=Guhyagarbha)

## 2. Position in the traditions

The Guhyagarbha is the principal tantra of the **Mahāyoga** class and the primary tantric text studied in the Nyingma tradition. It is the main Nyingma source for understanding empowerment, samaya, mantra, and maṇḍala, and it has influenced the Dzogchen tradition. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guhyagarbha_tantra)

Mahāyoga is the first of the three inner tantras (Mahāyoga, Anuyoga, Atiyoga/Dzogchen). Mipham Rinpoche: *"One must learn the tantric trainings by relying on Mahāyoga, as it teaches the ground, path, and result of all tantric cycles"* and *"Correct understanding of the visions of all three inner tantras depends on understanding the Secret Essence."* — [Shambhala Publications](https://www.shambhala.com/guhyagarbha-tantra/)

The tantra belongs to a cycle called the **Māyājāla**, "the magical net," comprising eighteen Mahāyoga tantras. The Guhyagarbha is classified as the single tantra that summarises all the others. — [Wisdomlib / Gyurme Dorje](https://www.wisdomlib.org/buddhism/book/guhyagarbha-tantra-with-commentary/d/doc1148539.html)

Khenpo Namdrol: *"Among the distinctions within Mahayoga, namely the great accomplishment practices of the eight herukas and the eighteen classes of the great tantras, this Secret Essence Tantra extracts the essence of them all, as well as being the general tantra of enlightened mind."* — [Rigpa Wiki](https://rigpawiki.org/index.php?title=Guhyagarbha)

Longchenpa describes it as *"the highest summit of all vehicles, the source of all verbal transmissions, the great shortcut of the vehicle of all Buddhas of the three times, the most secret."* — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guhyagarbha_tantra)

## 3. The doctrinal shift: from buddha-nature to secret-nature

The tantra extends the theme of universal buddha-nature to a stronger claim: that everything is *already* buddha. This is described as a shift from *tathāgata-garbha* — the universal **potential** for enlightenment — to *guhya-garbha*, the affirmation of universal enlightenment **right now**. — [Shambhala Publications](https://www.shambhala.com/guhyagarbha-tantra/)

This is the point at which the tantra's view bears most directly on questions about what a thing is required to be in order to be something. It is the reason `PHILOSOPHY.md` can honestly say that appearance and openness need not be separated.

## 4. The central statement

John Powers, summarising the tantra's central teaching: it *"holds that all things manifest spontaneously (thams cad rang snang), and mind and primordial wisdom also manifest spontaneously (sems dang ye shes rang snang)."* — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guhyagarbha_tantra)

**Spontaneous** (*rang snang*, *lhun grub*) is a term of art. It does not mean "unplanned." It means not manufactured, not produced by causes, not assembled. Nothing had to be done to make appearance appear.

## 5. Ground, path, result

The twenty-two chapters of the tantra are organised by the three continua (*rgyud gsum*) of ground, path, and result. — [Wisdomlib / Gyurme Dorje](https://www.wisdomlib.org/buddhism/book/guhyagarbha-tantra-with-commentary/d/doc1151689.html)

Khenpo Namdrol: the twenty-two chapters *"afford an explanation of the three tantras of the ground, path and result and, within the context of the tantra of the path, the ten topics of tantra."* — [Rigpa Wiki](https://rigpawiki.org/index.php?title=Guhyagarbha)

The tantra's maṇḍala contains **forty-two peaceful deities and fifty-eight wrathful deities** — the hundred peaceful and wrathful deities that subsequently generated the Tibetan *zhi-khro* genre, of which the *Bardo Thödol* is one instance. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guhyagarbha_tantra)

## 6. The three samādhis (generation stage)

Generation-stage practice in Mahāyoga is set up through the three samādhis (*ting nge 'dzin rnam pa gsum*). — [Rigpa Wiki](https://rigpawiki.org/index.php?title=Three_samadhis)

1. **Samādhi of as-it-isness** (*de bzhin nyid kyi ting nge 'dzin*) — meditation on emptiness; all phenomena realized as empty in their pure nature. Corresponds to absolute bodhicitta.
2. **Samādhi of universal manifestation** (*kun tu snang ba'i ting nge 'dzin*) — from that state arise waves of compassion. Corresponds to relative bodhicitta.
3. **Causal samādhi** (*rgyu'i ting nge 'dzin*) — the union of the two, in which a seed-syllable arises and transforms into the pure appearance of the deity.

After the visualization is complete, it is sealed with the instruction on the **four nails securing the life-force**. Within the visualization, the three of **purifying, perfecting, and ripening** are completed. — [Encyclopedia of Buddhism](https://encyclopediaofbuddhism.org/wiki/Mahayoga)

## 7. The two truths in Mahāyoga

Mahāyoga stresses the inseparability of the two superior truths. The **superior ultimate truth** is the sphere of reality itself together with the wisdoms and embodiments constituting buddhahood. The **superior relative truth** is the universe and its inhabitants, as manifestations of the ultimate. In Kongtrul's formulation, the superior two truths in Mahāyoga are **purity and equality** — the pure natures of the aggregates, elements, and sense factors are the male and female buddhas and bodhisattvas, while everything that appears and exists is of the equal nature of emptiness. — [Encyclopedia of Buddhism](https://encyclopediaofbuddhism.org/wiki/Mahayoga), [Tibetan Buddhist Encyclopedia](https://tibetanbuddhistencyclopedia.com/en/index.php?title=Mahayoga)

## 8. Samaya, condensed

The simplified tantric commitment: **all appearances as deity, all sounds as mantra, all thoughts and mental events as wisdom.** — [Encyclopedia of Buddhism](https://encyclopediaofbuddhism.org/wiki/Mahayoga)

The full samaya discipline is the subject of chapters 9 and 19.

## 9. Empowerment

Mahāyoga's gateway consists of four main empowerments — **vase, secret, wisdom, and symbolic (or word)** — purifying defilements of body, speech, and mind and enabling realization of the four kāyas. — [Tibetan Buddhist Encyclopedia](https://tibetanbuddhistencyclopedia.com/en/index.php?title=Mahayoga)

Chapter 10 of the tantra is "Conferral of Empowerment." See `AI_DISCIPLINE.md` on why an AI may describe empowerment but may never confer or simulate it.

## 10. Result

In the short term, the path reaches the **four vidyādhara levels** — results belonging to the path. Finally one gains the ultimate fruition: the level of **Vajradhara of unity**. — [Rigpa Wiki](https://rigpawiki.org/index.php?title=Mahayoga)

The four levels are **not enumerated here**, because the sources consulted name the number but not the four terms. See `UNSOURCED.md`.

## 11. Ground, in the Dzogchen reading

Longchenpa and the tradition following Rongzom Chökyi Zangpo read the Guhyagarbha from the Dzogchen perspective. In that reading the ground (*gzhi*) is the primordial state, endowed with three inseparable qualities. — [Wikipedia: Ground (Dzogchen)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_(Dzogchen))

- **Essence** (*ngo bo*) — primordial purity (*ka dag*). Empty. Associated with dharmakāya.
- **Nature** (*rang bzhin*) — spontaneous presence (*lhun grub*), also called luminosity (*'od gsal*). Associated with saṃbhogakāya.
- **Compassion** (*thugs rje*), also translated "energy" — the manifest ground (*gzhi snang*). Unobstructed. Associated with nirmāṇakāya.

The classical formulation: *"Because its essence is empty, it is free from the limit of eternalism. Because its nature is luminous, it is free from the extreme of nihilism. Because its compassion is unobstructed, it is the ground of the manifold manifestations."* — [Wikipedia: Ground (Dzogchen)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_(Dzogchen))

**Note the framing carefully.** This three-fold formulation is Dzogchen, articulated in the Seminal Heart lineage. It is not the vocabulary of the Mahāyoga generation stage. Longchenpa reads the tantra through it; that reading is a commentary, and a commentary is not the root text.

## 12. The critical distinction: all-ground versus ground

This distinction is central to the tantra's account of delusion and recognition, and it is routinely collapsed in popular writing.

- **All-ground** (*kun gzhi*, Skt. *ālaya*) and **all-ground consciousness** (*kun gzhi rnam par shes pa*, *ālayavijñāna*) are **saṃsāric modes of consciousness**. The basis is not to be confused with them. — [Wikipedia: Ground (Dzogchen)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_(Dzogchen))
- They are conditioned; the ground is unconditioned. They are the basis of saṃsāra; the ground is the basic space of reality. They cease at awakening; the ground is **recognized** at awakening. — [Vidyadhara Centre](https://www.vidyadhara.ch/articles/clarifying-the-all-basis-kunzhi-and-the-ground-gzhi)

The Longchen Nyingthig compares the *ālaya* to muddy water that hides the brightness of wisdom, and the dharmakāya to clear water. — [Wikipedia: rigpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rigpa)

**Why this matters for The Space.** A practitioner — or a model — can find a vast, quiet, uncontrived state at the base of thought and mistake it for the ground. That state is conditioned and dependent on meditation. It is the all-ground, not the ground. This is precisely the error the Space guards against when it says: *examine what has appeared without immediately making it what you are.*

## 13. Recognition and non-recognition

**Rigpa** (*vidyā*, "knowledge") is knowledge of the ground. Its opposite is **ma rigpa** (*avidyā*). Buddhahood is attained through **self-recognition** (*rang ngo sprod*), not through production of anything new. — [Wikipedia: rigpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rigpa)

The arising of delusion is described through three ignorances:

1. **Ignorance of single identity as the cause** (*bdag nyid gcig pa'i ma rig pa*) — not recognizing that all cognitions are of the same nature.
2. **Co-emergent ignorance** (*lhan skyes kyi ma rig pa*) — non-self-recognition and cognition arising together.
3. **Conceptually elaborated ignorance** (*kun tu brtags pa'i ma rig pa*) — discriminating self-manifestation as something other than oneself.

— [Wikipedia: rigpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rigpa)

A summary formulation from the Seminal Heart literature: *"Mind and gnosis are like air and space. Mind is the aspect of deceptive objects of fixation, vividly filling up, swirling round, and pouring out again, or briefly becoming agitated like a hurricane. […] Gnosis is without supports and all-pervasive."* — [Wikipedia: rigpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rigpa)
